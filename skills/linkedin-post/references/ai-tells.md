# AI tells

Readers who use AI a lot spot AI text very accurately (92.7% true positive, 4% false positive; Russell et al., ACL 2025).
Readers who think a post "sounds like AI" rate it lower. But a "make it not sound like AI" rewrite made posts look MORE
like AI (Joos et al., preprint, 194 LinkedIn users). So: fix substance first, then the tells below.

## Part A: evidence-backed (rewrite these)

1. **AI-associated vocabulary** (Kobak et al. 2025; Russell et al. 2025). One in a paragraph is fine; two or more,
   rewrite in plain words:
   delve, testament, crucial, pivotal, showcase, underscore, highlight (as a verb about meaning), meticulous,
   intricate, realm, tapestry, landscape (figurative), robust, comprehensive, seamless, leverage, foster, harness,
   unlock, elevate, empower, navigate (figurative), notably, fundamentally, ultimately, invaluable, game-changer.
2. **Trailing "-ing" analysis clauses** (Reinhart et al., PNAS 2025): "..., highlighting the importance of X",
   "..., ensuring that ...", "..., making it a ...". Split into a plain sentence or cut.
3. **Heavy nominalisation:** "the implementation of", "the optimization of", "the utilization of" → use the verb.
4. **Reflexive lists of three** everywhere ("faster, cheaper, better"). One natural triad per post at most.
5. **Over-explaining and hedging stacks:** "it's worth noting", "it's important to remember", "perhaps", "could
   potentially". Say it once, plainly.
6. **Flawless but generic content:** no scene, no number, no opinion. Fix by adding the author's specific detail (rule 6).

## Part B: folklore (avoid cheaply, never call it proof)

- Em dashes above about one per 100 words.
- "It's not X, it's Y" / "not just X, but Y" / "isn't about X, it's about Y".
- Reveal bridges: "The result?", "The catch?", "Here's the thing", "Here's what/how", "Plot twist:".
- Staccato stacks: "Short. Punchy. Done." and one-word paragraphs.
- Announced candour: "Let me be honest", "Real talk", "Unpopular opinion:".
- Openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "Have you ever wondered", "Most people don't realize".
- Closers: "Thoughts?", "Agree?", "What do you think?", "Let me know in the comments", "Tag someone who".
- Emoji bullets on every line; Unicode bold on whole paragraphs. (A house style with Unicode bold for the hook and
  short section headers is fine in moderation: put it in voice-profile.md.)

## Part C: do not flag

- Regional English (Indian, Irish, any other), natural idioms, casual asides, parentheses, light humour.
- Detectors misflag non-native English; never "correct" a voice toward generic American corporate English.
